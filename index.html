STEP 01

Write your guess at the trigger. One sentence.

The first row is the one before you exist. A number came back wrong. A colleague said something that stuck. A parent got a diagnosis. People don’t wake up wanting what you sell. They wake up having had something happen, and then they go looking. Don’t ask why they chose you. Ask what was going on the week they started looking.

Keep it. When you talk to real customers you’ll find out, and the gap between your guess and their answer is usually the most useful thing on the page.

STEP 02 + 03

Name every row by the question they’re holding. Then fill four columns.

A new row starts when the question in the customer’s head changes. Not when your process hands off, and not when a system logs something. Five to nine rows covers most businesses. Write every column in the customer’s words, not yours.

The lesson gives the seven row questions and the detail behind rows one, two and seven, plus what the month-two and month-fourteen members are working out. Cells it doesn’t cover are left blank rather than made up. A month-six row is missing on purpose: after the signature, Upstream’s map stopped.
